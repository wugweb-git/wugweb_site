# Vary span and ground, never boxes

A case-study body reads as designed when it holds a few things rigidly constant (gap, type scale, radius, text measure, the studio's own type and navigation) and varies only three things between consecutive blocks: how many grid columns the block spans, which type tier carries the text (label, statement, body), and, once or twice per page, the ground colour. That is what twelve leading studio pages actually do: none repeats "heading, paragraph, equal grid", each works from a vocabulary of 5 to 9 module types, and almost none puts a list of points into equal cards. Published guidance agrees with the observation: a set of homogeneous text points with no link target does not meet the definition of a card, so it belongs in ruled rows, a definition list, a lead plus support, or plain large type. Uneven counts are solved by choosing the form from the count (promote one item, mix widths, stack rows, or leave columns empty on purpose), not by padding content to three. The client's brand should enter on bounded surfaces inside the dark studio frame (screen backdrops, large numerals, rules, a dark tint), with the true brand hex kept for grounds and the client's own assets and a lifted "ink" value derived for anything drawn on the dark ground, because WCAG gives brand colours no exemption beyond the logo itself. The evidence has three grades and the report keeps them apart: studio patterns are observed in served HTML on 2026-11-19 but were never rendered, so anything visual is inferred from class names; the accessibility limits are normative; the per-count tables, pacing counts and the colour-lifting method are inference built for this report. Evidence is thinnest on mobile behaviour, on how studios present very large screen sets, on tinted grounds and duotones, and on device-less screens versus mockups, where no study or critic of standing was found.

## Twelve studios, two families, and almost no cards

The observations in this section come from reading the served HTML of one case-study page per studio on 2026-11-19: element order, visible character counts, image dimensions and layout class names. No page was rendered in a browser. Element order, counts and spans are therefore observed; any behaviour deduced from a class name (sticky, masonry, carousel, scroll fill) is inference; pixel spacing, hover and scroll behaviour and every mobile layout are unverified.

The pages split into two families, and this split is itself an inference from the observed structures. **Media-first** pages keep prose out of the body. Pentagram's Alpha Bank runs **46 media figures with 7 heading-size pull statements of 129 to 192 characters** interleaved at irregular intervals, no captions and no section headings, and the 12 narrative paragraphs sit after the whole stream ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/alpha-bank)). Wolff Olins puts the entire narrative in the header beside a "More Info" control and makes the body a media-only grid with large gaps ([Wolff Olins](https://www.wolffolins.com/work/decathlon)); Collins shows one 183-character opening statement and moves 13 paragraphs and the credits into a separate story sheet ([Collins](https://www.wearecollins.com/case-studies/figma)). **Chaptered editorial** pages use labelled chapters with one short paragraph each and a different media arrangement per chapter. Koto's Bolt has five chapters, each a mono uppercase label plus exactly one paragraph of 190 to 320 characters, then media ([Koto](https://koto.studio/projects/bolt)); Area 17 runs 298 to 413 characters per text block ([Area 17](https://area17.com/clients/fondation-cartier)); Fantasy 470 to 530 ([Fantasy](https://fantasy.co/work/art-basel)); Huge 95 to 218 ([Huge](https://www.hugeinc.com/case-study/lpga)). Instrument is the outlier at 795 and 874 characters ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/work/oura-smart-ring)). Across the chaptered family the working norm is **one paragraph of roughly 190 to 530 characters per chapter**, with the chapter heading split into two tiers: a tiny label that names the chapter and a separate statement-size line that carries the point.

### Points are set as type on the grid

The studios express a point as number, label, sentence and rule placed on the page grid. Fantasy sets four objectives as full-width rows with a top hairline, the number in a 1-column cell and the sentence in a 5-column cell starting at column 7, leaving columns 2 to 6 empty ([Fantasy](https://fantasy.co/work/art-basel)). Work and Co gives "01." its own 3 to 4 column cell beside an h1-size title, then 2 to 4 labelled sub-blocks, with a mini index of the three takeaways ahead of them ([Work and Co](https://work.co/clients/aesop/)). Instrument's "The Challenge:", "Our Approach:" and "The Impact:" are run-in labels followed by one 260 to 280 character statement at headline size with no body text in the module, and its three results are three plain lines of headline-size type ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/work/oura-smart-ring)). Area 17 sets "+400k" and "+50%" as bare numerals over a one-line label, each 3 to 4 of 12 columns, no container ([Area 17](https://area17.com/clients/fondation-cartier)). Clay pairs one square image with two side notes of about 100 characters ([Clay](https://clay.global/work/discover)). Order spreads a three-part idea across three consecutive image captions of three or four words each ([Order](https://order.design/project/aidkit)). Metadata is consistently a definition list of term and value pairs at Pentagram, Collins and Metalab, not chips ([Metalab](https://www.metalab.com/work/pitch)). Boxes appear only for results: Huge uses three rounded cards and Fantasy uses stat slides in a carousel. No annotated screens with callouts and no overlapping layers were found, but both would be invisible in HTML if they exist inside flattened images.

### Rhythm comes from width, type tier and ground

Order's AidKit body is the clearest specimen: 41 modules on a 12-column grid whose widths run 9 right-aligned, 8, 3, 8, 4, 4, 4, 12, 6 + 6, 2 + 8, 7 + 4, 4 + 7 and so on, with **a full 12-column image returning 13 times** as a reset ([Order](https://order.design/project/aidkit)). Area 17 uses three text widths on one page: headlines at 9 to 11 columns, body at a centred 6, metrics at 3 to 4 ([Area 17](https://area17.com/clients/fondation-cartier)). Instrument never places two text modules back to back without media between them, apart from a heading and its own paragraph ([Instrument](https://www.instrument.com/work/oura-smart-ring)). Clay runs white sections for the first half and switches to black at the full-screen brand image, staying black to the end ([Clay](https://clay.global/work/discover)), which suggests ground change is a once-per-page chapter signal, not a per-block device. Koto varies density by chapter with 1, 3, 7 and 1 media items, and mixes fixed frames at 16:9, 4:5, 1:1 and 5:3 ([Koto](https://koto.studio/projects/bolt)).

### Many screens and odd counts are absorbed by form, not by a bigger grid

Many screens are handled by motion and grouping. Fantasy's body is almost entirely video; Instrument carries 4 embedded videos and 3 inline clips against 3 still galleries. Area 17 uses four masonry groups, one of phone-proportioned 1000 by 1675 frames, plus looping media in 6-column halves ([Area 17](https://area17.com/clients/fondation-cartier)). Collins offers "Explore the gallery" with grid and stack views outside the story ([Collins](https://www.wearecollins.com/case-studies/figma)). Order groups images in named sets and lets one caption introduce each set ([Order](https://order.design/project/aidkit)). The inference: the grouping unit is a flow or topic with one caption, phone screens stay tall inside masonry or strips, and a separate full gallery shows everything without lengthening the story. This is the weakest part of the studio evidence, because **no page with 30 or more distinct UI screens was read**; Metalab, the most relevant studio, renders its body in the browser.

For uneven counts the observed tactics are mixed-width rows (Koto's 7-image chapter draws on full-width 16:9 and half-width 4:5 frames), rows that deliberately do not sum to 12 (Order's 7 + 4, 2 + 8, 8 + 3), a size modifier on single items (Pentagram marks 3 of 46 as half), carousels with a fixed minimum slide width (Fantasy), one item per ruled row (Fantasy, Instrument), and a last cell that differs in kind: Huge fills a row of three with two numeric cards and one sentence-only card ([Huge](https://www.hugeinc.com/case-study/lpga)). No studio documents a rule for odd counts, and the exact arrangement of Koto's seven images was not confirmed visually. Empty columns read as intent when the alignment is consistent; a hole reads as a mistake only inside an otherwise uniform grid.

## Published guidance turns the craft into checks

The principles below are guidance and practitioner opinion, with a small core of research and documented CSS behaviour. The rules drawn from them in the table are this report's synthesis; no source publishes a per-count table or a pacing count.

On **cards versus lists**, NN/g defines a card as a container that acts as a linked preview of a single concept, suited to browsing heterogeneous content, and advises against cards when readers scan for a detail, compare items, or when items are homogeneous ([NN/g, Cards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/)). The support is the author's qualitative eyetracking shown through a single gaze plot, with no sample size, so this is guidance with supporting observation, not a measured effect. A case-study points block has no per-item link and its items are alike, so by that definition it is a list. NN/g's list guidance adds that numbers belong only where order or count matters, that three or more items justify a vertical list while fewer read better inline, that every list needs a lead-in, and that items should share grammatical form and similar length without repeating opening words ([NN/g, Bulleted Lists](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/presenting-bulleted-lists/)). The firmest research is on scanning: eyetracking, including a 2017 study with 47 participants, shows F-shaped and layer-cake patterns in which headings and the first words of lines carry the page ([NN/g, F-Shaped Pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)). Nothing in the guidance asks for the same number of points in every section; padding to three conflicts with the rules to cut content and keep items of equal importance. That conclusion is inferred, since no source addresses forced symmetry directly.

On **balance**, weight rises with size, darker value, contrast and subject interest, and one dominant element is balanced by two or more lighter ones ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/06/design-principles-compositional-balance-symmetry-asymmetry/)). NN/g limits large attention-grabbing elements to two per view and type sizes to three ([NN/g, Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)). The grid failure is precise: a lone item in the last row at a different width reads as accidentally emphasised ([Every Layout, Switcher](https://every-layout.dev/layouts/switcher/)). Two documented fixes exist. Promote the first item to full width on an odd count in two columns and let its inner layout turn horizontal at a container width of 400px ([Dave Rupert](https://daverupert.com/2022/07/solving-the-dangler-conundrum-with-has-and-container-queries)), or centre leftovers in a three-column grid, which the author prefers to widening because a wide leftover gains unintended emphasis ([CSS In Real Life](https://css-irl.info/controlling-leftover-grid-items/)). Subgrid aligns the inner parts of sibling cells when lengths differ ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/css-subgrid)).

On **rhythm**, Müller-Brockmann's rule is that elements span one to four grid fields and fewer distinct sizes read calmer ([excerpt](https://neugraphic.com/muller-brockmann/muller-brockmann-text2.html)); Every Layout caps the measure at 60ch within a 45 to 75 character range ([Every Layout, Axioms](https://every-layout.dev/rudiments/axioms/)). On **mixed or bento grids**, the only hard facts are CSS behaviour: auto-placement leaves holes when a spanning item does not fit, and `grid-auto-flow: dense` backfills them by breaking visual order away from DOM order, which MDN says suits unordered galleries and not sequenced content ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_grid_layout/Auto-placement_in_grid_layout)). Everything else about bento grids is vendor opinion: 4 to 8 tiles, size by importance, constant background, radius, padding and type scale, and no grid for a single message or a long feature list ([SaaSFrame](https://www.saasframe.io/blog/designing-bento-grids-that-actually-work-a-2026-practical-guide)). No usability evidence compares mixed and uniform grids. On **recomposition**, the sources favour components that choose their arrangement from their own container width and item count, with two or three deliberate states and no in-between wraps ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/new-responsive)), and type that interpolates between a small-screen and a large-screen scale ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/designing-with-fluid-type-scales/)). No source gives rules for exactly 375, 768 and 1200.

| Check | Test | Basis | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card eligibility | A block of text points with no per-item link target is never rendered as cards | NN/g card definition | Guidance |
| Form from count | 1 point is a statement; 2 are a sentence or pair; 3 or more parallel fragments are ruled rows or a list with a lead-in; ordered items are numbered; term and explanation pairs are a definition list | NN/g lists; studio rows | Guidance plus inference |
| Orphan | For n items in c columns, recomputed at each width: if n mod c is 1, promote one item or reject; any other remainder, centre the last row or redistribute spans | Every Layout, Rupert, Barker | Inference from opinion |
| Promotion side | Promote the first item, not the last | Barker and Rupert | Inference |
| Weight | At most two elements per viewport use the largest size step; at most three type sizes per view | NN/g hierarchy | Guidance |
| Alternation | No two consecutive blocks share both type and span | Order, Instrument | Inference from observation |
| Text then media | Any block over about 300 characters is followed by media; after two or three partial-width blocks comes a full-width one | Instrument, Order | Inference; no source gives a count |
| Chapter copy | One paragraph per chapter, about 190 to 530 characters | Five chaptered studio pages | Observed range used as a rule |
| Span budget | At most four block widths on the 1200 grid | Müller-Brockmann | Inference |
| Measure | Body text at or below 60ch at 1200; text beside media at 768 only if the column keeps about 45 characters | Every Layout | Opinion; the 768 threshold is inference |
| Mosaic eligibility | 4 to 8 items whose natural sizes differ; text-only cells never; exactly one dominant cell; spans from asset aspect ratio | SaaSFrame, MDN | Opinion plus inference |
| Hole | Sum of cell areas equals columns times rows used, else change a span or the form | MDN auto-placement | Inference from spec |
| Order | Dense flow only for unordered screen walls; sequenced points keep DOM order | MDN | Spec |
| List grammar | Items in one list share a grammatical form and the first two words of each are unique | NN/g lists, F-pattern | Guidance |
| Ground change | At most one or two per page, at a chapter boundary | Clay | Inference from one page |

At phone width the measure is set by the screen, so rhythm there has to come from vertical spacing, type tier and the alternation of text with full-width media; column variation is unavailable. Tablet is where odd counts hurt most, which is why the orphan check must be recomputed per width. Both statements are inference: the studio pages were not rendered at any width, so there is no observation of how leading studios recompose on a phone.

## Client colour enters on bounded surfaces, with WCAG as the hard limit

Studios split on theming, on a sample of about eight pages that supports "these patterns exist" and not "most studios do this". Ragged Edge sets exactly one client colour as the page ground and holds its own text colour constant: `#9FE870` for Wise, `#FFD7F0` for Monzo, `#F1E9DD` for Gaia, all with `#181F1F` text and the studio's own navigation, typefaces and chapter structure ([Ragged Edge, Wise](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/wise); [Monzo](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/monzo); [Gaia](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/gaia)). Collins, Koto, Pentagram and Wolff Olins, as inspected, do not theme the page at all; the client palette arrives only inside the media, and Collins' whole theming surface is a ground and foreground token pair ([Collins](https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/figma/)). Metalab tags the elements that follow a per-page theme with `data-themed` attributes for background, colour, fill and stroke ([Metalab](https://www.metalab.com/work/upwork)). In every case the studio keeps its navigation, type family, grid and section vocabulary; at most one or two colours vary. Tinted section grounds, duotone images and coloured numerals could not be confirmed from HTML and need a screenshot pass.

Ragged Edge's method works with one fixed text colour only because all three sampled grounds are light: the pairs compute to 11.36:1, 12.94:1 and 13.90:1 under the WCAG 2 formula. A dark neutral system cannot copy it directly, and a full-page saturated ground would remove the dark frame that identifies the studio. The translation, which is inference, is to give each case three derived roles from one brand hex and check each separately: a **ground** for bounded bands and screen backdrops, an **ink** for numerals, rules and icons drawn on the dark ground, and a **tint** mixed into the dark ground for section surfaces.

The limits are normative. WCAG 2.2 requires **4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text** (at least 18 point, or 14 point bold), ratios are not rounded, and the logotype exception covers only logos: text that has low contrast because of corporate identity guidelines is not exempt ([W3C, SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Meaningful graphics and control boundaries need **3:1**, with exemptions for logos, screenshots, photographs and pure decoration ([W3C, SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). The table shows what that means on a dark ground. The ratios were calculated for this report with the WCAG formula and rechecked; the brand values are illustrative.

| Pair | Ratio | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bright green `#9FE870` as ink on `#191919` | 11.93:1 | Passes for all text |
| Red `#E50914` as ink on `#191919` | 3.67:1 | Large text and graphics only |
| Blue `#0000FF` as ink on `#191919` | 2.05:1 | Fails everything |
| Navy `#1A1F71` as ink on `#191919` | 1.24:1 | Effectively invisible |
| White text on a red `#E50914` ground | 4.79:1 | Passes; black at 4.38:1 fails |
| White text on an orange `#FF6900` ground | 2.89:1 | Fails; black at 7.27:1 passes |
| Studio text `#f4f4f4` on `#191919` | 15.99:1 | Passes |
| Studio muted `#bababa` on `#2b2b2b` | 7.30:1 | Passes |

Two consequences follow. Dark brand colours cannot be used raw as ink, and the right text colour on a brand ground has to be calculated per colour. Current guidance for deriving the variants is OKLCH, whose lightness is perceptual and whose hue holds when chroma changes, using relative colour syntax such as `oklch(from var(--accent) calc(l + 0.1) c h)` ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)), and `color-mix(in oklab, ...)` to mix the brand colour into the page ground for tinted surfaces, with `@property` registration so an invalid per-case value falls back safely ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/baseline-in-action-color-theme)). Holding hue and chroma and raising lightness until the ink passes is this report's inference from those techniques; **no source publishes a tested algorithm for lifting a dark brand colour on a dark ground while keeping it recognisable**, so results need an eye check. APCA is worth running as a second test on any pair that only just passes, because its author states WCAG 2 is least reliable for dark colours, but APCA is not a W3C Recommendation and cannot replace the WCAG gate ([Myndex](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-introduction)).

### Icons, type and branded visuals belong to the exhibit, not the chrome

NN/g holds that an icon must first communicate meaning, that very few are universally understood, that a visible label should accompany every icon, and that a concept with no obvious icon within about five seconds should not get one ([NN/g, Icon Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). That research is from 2014 and concerns interface icons; applying it to editorial pages is inference, but it argues against rows of invented icons above abstract points. W3C treats an image that illustrates adjacent text without adding information as decorative, to be given an empty alt ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decorative/)); a stand-alone meaningful icon needs 3:1. The separation rule, studio icons for controls and page chrome, client icons only as content inside a figure, specimen or screen at their native stroke and colour, is inference; no source addresses it.

For identity work, the pages read sequence context, idea, identity elements, applications and outcome. Ragged Edge's Wise page gives each element a short named block of a label plus one or two sentences, running colour, logo, bespoke typeface, verbal identity and a graphic asset system, then product, then results ([Ragged Edge](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/wise)). Pentagram's Hyphen puts colour after type ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/hyphen/story)), so the order follows whichever element carries the idea. For product work the brand enters through the interface itself ([Metalab](https://www.metalab.com/work/upwork)). No studio inspected replaces its own typeface with the client's; client type appears as specimen. No readable palette block or icon-set block was found on any page, so how leaders present those two specimens is undocumented here. The recommendation to print typeface names and palette values as text beside the images is inference, on accessibility grounds.

The case for device-less screens on a brand-coloured backdrop is the thinnest finding in this report. It rests on practitioner and vendor opinion retrieved partly as search summaries ([Screenhance](https://screenhance.com/blog/portfolio-screenshots)), no study, and no named critic; Metalab opens its Upwork case with a photographed laptop. The backdrop remains the best single place to spend client colour in a dark system, because it is bounded, sits behind the client's own product and carries no studio text. That is a reasoned inference. What is normative: the screenshot is exempt from contrast rules, but any caption the studio sets over or beside it is ordinary text. Theming with client colour also belongs inside the client's sign-off, and should be dropped for anonymised work because a palette can identify the client; the only substantive source for permission practice is UK legal commentary, not legal advice ([Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/client-logos-website-permission/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Designed, not templated" does not call for more block types or more decoration: the studio pages use a handful of modules and almost no containers. What separates them from a template is that the content picks the form. Count, text length and asset aspect ratio are inputs, and the layout is the output, which means a resolver can produce this quality mechanically if it treats those three as inputs and runs the checks above on the resolved block list. It also means the writing and the layout cannot be tuned separately: sections with 3, then 5, then 1 point produce the alternation of dense and open that rhythm needs, and forcing equal counts removes it.

Three pieces of work would turn the inferred parts into observed ones. A rendered screenshot pass of the same studio pages at 375, 768 and 1200 would settle mobile recomposition, tinted grounds, duotones and device framing, none of which HTML can show. A read of one client-rendered, screen-heavy product case would fill the gap on 30 or more screens. And the brand-ink lift needs a small test set of real client colours against the dark ground, judged by eye as well as by ratio, before it is trusted as a build gate. Until then, the WCAG thresholds and the CSS placement behaviour are the only parts of this report that should fail a build; everything else should warn.
